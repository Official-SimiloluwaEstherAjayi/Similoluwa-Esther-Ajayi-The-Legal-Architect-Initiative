The Legal Architect

Law as infrastructure,
not just enforcement.

I am interested in the systems behind legal outcomes — institutions, contracts, technology, governance and the architecture through which law either creates durable value or fails to.

This is where I think out loud and build. The record of what I have actually done sits on the professional record.

Start with the thesis

The Thesis

What I mean by architecture.

Most legal education teaches law as a body of rules and a set of remedies: here is the obligation, here is what happens when it is broken. That is true, and it is not the whole picture.

Law is also the thing that decides, before any dispute arises, who can enter a market, who can raise capital, whose name goes on an invention, and who even hears that an opportunity exists. Those are design questions. Someone answered them — often badly, often by accident, often by leaving the answer to whoever happened to be in the room.

That is the work I am interested in: the design, not only the remedy.

I

Structure beats effort

An institution that depends on one dedicated person is not an institution; it is that person. The test of a system is what survives their departure.

II

Access is a design choice

Who hears about an opportunity is not a matter of luck. It is decided by whatever channel exists, or fails to. Building the channel is legal work as much as drafting is.

III

Evidence before adjectives

Nothing on this site calls itself authoritative, elite or high-performing. Each project below states what exists today and what does not yet.

Why this exists

A gap that is structural, not personal.

Law students around me are academically serious and often better read than they are given credit for. What many of them do not have is any reliable route to the things that turn study into a career: which firms take interns, when applications open, what a good memorandum looks like, how a committee is supposed to hand over to the next one.

That information exists. It simply moves through private networks, which means it reaches whoever is already connected. The gap is not a talent problem. It is an infrastructure problem, and infrastructure can be built.

Projects

What is actually being built.

Each one states its stage honestly. A project in design is described as a project in design, and the live files are linked so the claim can be checked rather than taken on trust.

01 In design

Standard Drafting Precedents Project

A shared, structured collection of model drafting precedents across ten practice areas, built so that several committees can contribute to the same framework rather than each keeping its own private folder. The longer aim is to make Northern and Southern procedural variations visible side by side instead of discovered by accident.

CriminalTax Civil litigationProperty CommercialEstates & trusts M&AEnergy Intellectual propertyADR
What exists today
A ten-part master directory structure on shared drive space, set up for multi-committee collaboration.
What it is not yet
A registry. The precedents themselves are still being collected, and nothing here is nationally adopted or endorsed.

02 In build

Institutional Architecture Blueprint

An operating framework for how law student committees, chambers and directorates divide work, document it and hand it over — so that a new administration inherits a structure rather than a rumour of one.

What exists today
Organisational proposals drafted and submitted to establish independent operational directorates, with documentation and handover procedures attached.
What it is not yet
Fully adopted. Some elements are running at ABUAD and in LAWSAN South-West; the rest is proposal stage.
Inspect the files
03 Running

Opportunity Architecture

The design behind the ABUAD LSS Opportunity Office: two desks covering undergraduate and graduate stages, a tracking and dissemination routine, and a handover procedure so the office does not die with its founder.

What exists today
The office is operating, with both desks established and application sequences and portfolio registries in use.
What it is not yet
Adopted beyond the Law Students’ Society. Extension through the Students’ Representative Council to other ABUAD colleges is still being worked on.

Open Questions

What I do not have answers to yet.

These are the questions the reading and the writing keep circling. They are listed here because an honest laboratory shows its unfinished work.

One

Digital identity and privacy in emerging economies

What does meaningful consent mean where identity systems are a precondition for banking, schooling and subsidy, and opting out is not a real option?

Two

Where legal infrastructure actually fails

If law is infrastructure, it should be possible to say which part is load-bearing and which part is decorative. I do not yet have a clean test for telling them apart.

Three

African legal-technology ecosystems

Why so much legal technology built for African practice solves the drafting problem and not the access problem, and what would change if that were reversed.

Four

Reputation as a form of capital

Professional reputation behaves like an asset but is governed almost entirely by norms rather than rules. What happens when the platforms that hold it change their terms?

How this got here

2024

Systems for myself

Built study, note and file structures to handle the volume of law school. They were personal tools, nothing more.

2025

Testing them on other people

Shared the approach, mentored junior students, and found out which parts transferred and which only worked because I had made them.

The Opportunity Office, the LAWSAN South-West programme structures and the drafting precedents project — the same instinct applied to bodies rather than to a desk.
